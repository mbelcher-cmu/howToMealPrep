How it works

From goal to cart in three taps

No spreadsheets, no calculator, no guessing what fits.

Set your numbers

Your calorie and protein goal, weekly budget, meals and snacks a day, and anything you don't eat. We do the math so the calories always match the macros.

Get a week that fits

Our planner weighs every recipe against your goal, your budget and real prices at nearby stores, then picks the cheapest week that hits your numbers.

Shop, prep, repeat

Walk the store with a checklist in aisle order. Cook it all in one session. Next week reuses what you didn't finish.

Built for real meal preppers

Everything between "I should eat better" and Sunday prep

Re-plans as you type

Change the budget, the mood or a meal and the whole week updates instantly, still on target.

Split-week rotation

Two batches, one shop: meal A Monday to Wednesday, meal B the rest of the week. No seven days of the same thing.

Pantry that carries over

Rice, oil and spices you already own come off the list. Leftovers roll into next week's plan first.

Priced for your store

Enter your ZIP, pick the stores you shop at, and every item on your list carries that store's price. Fix any price from your receipt and the next plan uses it.

FAQ

Questions

Where do the prices come from?

During the pilot, prices are careful estimates for each store, and you can correct any price you see on your receipt. Live store prices are on the way.

Which stores work?

Aldi, Walmart, Giant Eagle and Trader Joe's in Pittsburgh, and Walmart and Aldi across the US. Enter your ZIP and we show the stores near you.

Does it work for vegetarian, high-protein or allergy-friendly diets?

Yes. Set your diet, allergies and foods you dislike, and every plan respects them while still hitting your numbers.

Do I need an account?

No. Your plan is saved on your device. Accounts that sync your plan across devices are coming for the pilot.

Is this medical or dietitian advice?

No. MacroAisle helps you plan food to the targets you choose. Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian about what those targets should be.
